# PR #80 (review roster, B1): Astra reviews

## Review of the roster change (Astra, 2026-10-05)

Verdict: NO-GO

The key change is sound for direct cache lookup, but it does not yet establish the full “by construction” claim. One High finding blocks; the remaining findings are Medium.

1. **High — Merge-queue carry-over can bypass the new roster requirement.**  
   **Location:** `docs/design/m0/13-artifact-graph.md:58`, interacting with lines 158–168 and 88–91.

   A queue generation can already reference roster A’s review when the required roster changes to B. Creating B’s review node prevents A’s result from satisfying B’s computation key, but the existing `carry_over` rule can still attest A’s review against the prospective tree: it requires patch equality and no conflict resolution, without checking the current roster. This path never needs to satisfy B’s review key. The commit-time checks in 06 cover subject, demand and independence, but not roster currency; an unchanged base also lets the merge CAS succeed.

   **Fix:** Bind carry-over eligibility to the currently required review identity, including its roster. A roster replacement must invalidate or supersede affected pending queue work, and merge admission must recheck that binding. Preserve carry-over across base changes when the required roster remains unchanged. Extend the fixture with a roster change after carry-over selection but before merge: merge must wait for the replacement review.

2. **Medium — Hashing the declared roster does not bind actual placement to it.**  
   **Location:** `docs/design/m0/13-artifact-graph.md:55–57`.

   Note 05 permits fallback after repeating placement checks, including independence when changing family. Those checks do not explicitly require the selected executor and verified family to match the roster hashed into this node. An otherwise authorized fallback therefore needs an explicit connection to the new-node rule.

   **Fix:** Require placement and accepted review provenance to match the pinned roster. A fallback that changes a member must instantiate the replacement node before launch; it cannot execute under the old key.

   **Both executor ID and family are appropriate here.** Family alone conflates distinct executors; executor ID alone requires an immutable, versioned family mapping to preserve the independence meaning. Derive the family from controller-controlled executor/routing information, consistent with 06:41–45, rather than accepting it as an independent caller assertion.

3. **Medium — The explanation of conclusions equivalence does not establish its claimed protection.**  
   **Location:** `docs/design/m0/13-artifact-graph.md:58–65`.

   Conclusions equivalence on `review←gate` and `review←evidence` is precisely a mechanism for skipping review execution despite changed input result IDs. Saying that equivalence never applies *to a review result* does not establish that this skip decision compares the consumer’s roster. The existing conditions list subject, demand, instrument and restrictions, but omit review params and generation.

   **Fix:** Explicitly limit this optimization to reviews with identical non-input computation identity, including roster params, definition version and computation generation. Only the permitted input edges may differ. Add a fixture with equal gate/evidence conclusions but a changed roster: the replacement review must run. A roster change already changes the params digest; it does not need an otherwise artificial generation bump.

4. **Medium — Replacement lacks a defined successor and retirement transition.**  
   **Location:** `docs/design/m0/13-artifact-graph.md:60–61` and `:218–219`.

   “Retired … explicitly” leaves the actor, recorded relationship and treatment of an in-flight attempt unspecified. New node identity alone does not close the old attempt’s publication under 05. Nor does it say how the replacement relates to successor rounds: outstanding evidence demands, owed inputs and their answers must survive reviewer replacement, while cumulative rails must not reset. Retiring a member-availability hold must not silently discharge those obligations.

   **Fix:** Define a controller-owned, recorded supersession transition under 03’s writer transaction, linking predecessor, replacement and retired hold IDs. Specify stopping/fencing outstanding attempts through 05, with normal settlement and accounting. Preserve successor inputs, unresolved obligations and cumulative counters; retire only waits made obsolete by the membership change. Expand the fixture definition to cover late output, replay after restart, and replacement while an owed input remains outstanding.

The added fixture targets the basic identity change correctly, but its current assertions can pass while these alternate reuse and lifecycle paths remain incorrect. No files were modified.
### Lead notes on round 1 (applied)

- **High (carry-over across a roster change):**
  - The `carry_over` node takes the currently required review identity, roster included, as an input.
  - It attests only when the carried review's identity equals it. Otherwise it demands the replacement review.
  - The merge's commit-time re-validation ([G4]) checks that binding.
  - Fixture: a roster change after carry-over was selected, but before the merge commits, makes the merge wait for the
    replacement.
- **Medium (placement bound to the roster):** 05 gains the rule that a review member places only on its pinned
  executor. A fallback that would change a member records the supersession and leases for the replacement, never
  under the old key. The roster pins both executor id and family, and the family comes from the controller's
  executor registry (06), never from a caller.
- **Medium (conclusions equivalence):** the reuse rule now also requires the consumer's non-input identity (kind,
  definition version, params including the roster, generation) to be identical; only the declared input edges may
  differ. Fixture: equal gate or evidence conclusions with a changed roster run the replacement.
- **Medium (supersession transition):** a roster change is one controller event in the writer transaction,
  `review.superseded{predecessor, replacement, retired_holds}`.
  - The replacement keeps the round and the inputs. Owed inputs, demands, answers and rails carry over.
  - In-flight attempts get `stop_requested{preempt}`: they are fenced and settled normally.
  - Only holds made obsolete are retired, and they are named in the event; a hold for an outstanding obligation moves
    to the replacement.
  - Replay reaches the same supersession.
  - The fixture covers late output, replay and an outstanding owed input.
