### Findings
- **H1** (BLOCKING, High: a task left stuck with no hold; pre-existing on main 8adbed7, but inside #433's promise):
  - **What happens:** before a build's or a review's adapter starts, `runner.attempt` calls `placer.launch` with the runner's own context, `r.ctx` (`internal/daemon/run.go:238`, `:350`). `launch` calls `dispatch.Observe(ctx, …)` (`internal/daemon/place.go:397`), which runs `d.Images.Resolve(ctx, card.Image)` (`internal/dispatch/launch.go:69`). Nothing bounds that resolve: `sandbox.Host.podman` adds no timeout. So a podman stuck on its storage lock blocks the attempt before `Start`'s bounded `resolveImage` is ever reached.
  - **Probe** (`switchImages.hang` set before the first pass, `resolveTimeout` = 50 ms): after 6 s and 6 passes, the build was still `claimed` with no hold and one run started. It stayed that way until the daemon's context ended, and even then no hold was raised.
  - **Why it matters here:** my first round's L2 said the build's launch was bounded. It was not; `Start`'s resolve comes after this one.
  - **Fix:** in `placer.launch`, observe under `context.WithTimeout(ctx, resolveTimeout)`. A hang then reads as the readiness's image miss (`wrong_owner`, `dispatch.ImageMiss`), which `imageReady` clears once podman answers, so it is bounded and visible. Alternatively, carry `errHost` through `Readiness` to get the bounded retry, which is consistent with `checkout`. Add a row: a hung resolve at launch is held, not left claimed.
- **L1** (OBLIGATION, Low): `imageReady` resolves under `dispatch.ReadinessBound` (5 s; `internal/daemon/place.go:279`), while `resolveTimeout` is 30 s. A podman that answers in 5–30 s passes `checkout` but never clears a `wrong_owner` hold. Use one bound for both, or state the difference.
