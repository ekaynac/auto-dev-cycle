## Review of #78 (Astra, 2026-10-05)

Verdict: NO-GO

- **High — D43 can exclude real security defects from blocking review.** `docs/plan/00-brainstorm-decisions.md:88`; `docs/plan/04-development-workflow.md:58`. “An unprivileged process that ignores a cooperative lock” covers concurrent writers, but does not clearly cover malicious API clients, forged adapter messages, or sandbox escapes where no lock exists. Applied to **every** contract PR, the sentence can excuse an authorization bypass because it involves neither honest use nor lock avoidance. Likewise, “detected, not prevented” is appropriate for verification snapshots, not a general relaxation of sandbox or authority boundaries. **Fix:** make unprivileged adversarial behavior explicitly in scope, with lock-ignoring writers as an example; scope detection-only handling to snapshot verification. Specify that excluded elevated privileges must be possessed **before** exploitation, and that D43 does not relax existing contracts or §4’s operator gate. Reachability establishes eligibility to block; severity must still be Critical/High.

D42 still says GitHub-hosted CI runs bubblewrap; 04 §8, line 130, still advertises Docker fallback. **Fix:** annotate the historical runner statement as superseded by #75 and align §8 with D42’s bubblewrap-only rule until M3.

**New finding — Low: qualify the excluded privileges as elevated.**  
`docs/plan/00-brainstorm-decisions.md:88`; `docs/plan/04-development-workflow.md:61`.

“Privileges the attacker must already hold before the exploit” correctly distinguishes prerequisites from privileges gained through exploitation, but omits **elevated**. Ordinary authentication, a limited supervisor grant or normal filesystem access are also pre-existing privileges. Read literally, the exclusion therefore overlaps the expressly included adversaries.

**Fix:** say “attacks requiring pre-existing elevated privileges outside the stated threat model,” and explicitly retain attacks starting with ordinary credentials or limited grants. Keep deliberate operator build overrides as a separate exclusion. This is nonblocking because the surrounding text expressly includes unprivileged adversaries and preserves existing authority and sandbox contracts.
